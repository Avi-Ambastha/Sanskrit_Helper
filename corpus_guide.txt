# SANSKRIT GRAMMAR RAG

## Corpus Expansion Guidelines

### 1. Use UTF-8 plain text

All corpus files should preferably be `.txt` files encoded as UTF-8.

This is important because the corpus contains Devanagari, Sanskrit diacritics, and transliteration.

GOOD:
जात्यस्वरितः
Kṣaipra
Praśliṣṭa
Udātta
Anudātta

AVOID:
Ksaipra -> when the original source actually contains Kṣaipra, unless the alternate spelling is deliberately retained for retrieval.

---

### 2. Preserve the original Sanskrit

Do not rewrite or "improve" Sanskrit source material.

GOOD:

उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य यदा संधिर्यणात्मकः ।
क्षैप्नामा तदा जात्यस्वरितः सर्वदा भवेत् ॥

If the source contains a spelling or grammatical form that looks unusual, preserve it unless you are explicitly creating a corrected edition.

The RAG should retrieve what the source actually says.

---

### 3. Keep the original source's terminology

Do not silently replace the source's terminology with modern terminology.

GOOD:

Jātyasvarita
Śuddha Svarita
Kṣaipra
Abhinihita
Praśliṣṭa

If an explanation is added, keep the source term and explain it separately.

GOOD:

Jātyasvarita (जात्यस्वरितः)

Explanation:
The source describes this as a Svarita that is naturally Svarita and similar to Udātta.

---

### 4. Keep one coherent concept together

This is one of the MOST IMPORTANT rules.

A rule, its explanation, and its examples should normally remain together.

GOOD:

क्षैप्रः

उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य यदा संधिर्यणात्मकः ।
क्षैप्रनामा तदा जात्यस्वरितः सर्वदा भवेत् ॥

Translation:
...

Example:
त्रि + अंबकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

Explanation:
...

BAD:

File/chunk 1:
उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य...

File/chunk 2:
Translation of the rule...

File/chunk 3:
त्रि + अंबकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

The second arrangement makes it harder for retrieval to understand that all three pieces belong together.

---

### 5. Preserve headings

Every important grammatical section should have a clear heading.

Example:

जात्योत्पत्तिः

Then its content.

Then:

क्षैप्रः

Then its content.

Then:

प्रश्लिष्टः

Then its content.

Then:

अभिनिहितः

Then its content.

Headings are extremely valuable for retrieval because technical Sanskrit terms often appear primarily in headings.

---

### 6. Preserve parent-child relationships

If one section introduces several concepts that are explained later, preserve that structure.

Example:

जात्योत्पत्तिः

A Jātya is produced when the relevant Sandhi belongs to one of:

* Abhinihita
* Kṣaipra
* Praśliṣṭa

Later:

क्षैप्रः
...

प्रश्लिष्टः
...

अभिनिहितः
...

Do NOT remove the parent section just because the individual definitions exist elsewhere.

The parent section tells the RAG how the concepts are related.

---

### 7. Keep examples with the rule they demonstrate

Example:

क्षैप्रः

Condition:
Udātta + Anudātta → Yaṇ

Example:
त्रि + अंबकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

This is better than putting all examples in a separate "Examples" section.

The retrieval system can then return the definition and its evidence together.

---

### 8. Keep Sanskrit examples exactly as the source gives them

Do not automatically normalize examples.

GOOD:

त्रि + अंबकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

If the source gives another spelling:

त्रि + अम्बकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

preserve that source form.

If you want to provide a normalized form, explicitly label it:

Source form:
...

Normalized form:
...

Do not silently substitute one for the other.

---

### 9. Keep translations attached to their Sanskrit rule

GOOD:

Sanskrit:
उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य...

Translation:
If an Udātta and Anudātta join to create Yaṇ...

Example:
त्रि + अंबकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

This gives the retriever both the Sanskrit and the semantic explanation.

---

### 10. Clearly distinguish source text from added explanation

If you manually add explanations to a source, label them.

GOOD:

SOURCE TEXT:
उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य...

SOURCE TRANSLATION:
...

EXPLANATION:
This means that...

EXAMPLE:
...

This prevents the model from confusing your explanation with the original author's words.

---

### 11. Do not mix sources without identifying them

If two books discuss Kṣaipra, keep their identities separate.

GOOD:

SOURCE: Svaramañjarī
SECTION: क्षैप्रः

...

SOURCE: Another Grammar Text
SECTION: ...

...

BAD:

Kṣaipra:
[material from five different books mixed together]

The second approach makes attribution and conflicting interpretations difficult.

---

### 12. Add source metadata when possible

At the beginning of each source file, use something like:

SOURCE:
Svaramañjarī

AUTHOR:
[author if known]

TOPIC:
Vedic Accent

LANGUAGE:
Sanskrit

SOURCE TYPE:
Grammar / Commentary / Reference

Then begin the actual text.

Example:

SOURCE: Svaramañjarī
TOPIC: Vedic Accent
LANGUAGE: Sanskrit

जात्योत्पत्तिः

...

---

### 13. Keep chapters/sections identifiable

If the source has a hierarchy, preserve it.

Example:

SOURCE: Book X

CHAPTER: Vedic Accents

SECTION: Svarita

SUBSECTION: Jātyasvarita

CONTENT:
...

This helps when the corpus becomes much larger.

---

### 14. Do not convert everything into question-answer pairs

You do NOT need to create:

Question:
What is Kṣaipra?

Answer:
Kṣaipra is...

Instead, preserve the original instructional/reference material.

The RAG can generate questions and answers dynamically.

Primary structure:

SOURCE MATERIAL
→ RETRIEVAL
→ LLM SYNTHESIS
→ ANSWER

not:

PREWRITTEN QUESTIONS
→ PREWRITTEN ANSWERS

---

### 15. Avoid unnecessary duplication

Do not copy the same rule into ten different places just to make retrieval easier.

BAD:

Kṣaipra definition repeated 10 times.

GOOD:

One authoritative section:

क्षैप्रः

...

If retrieval later needs aliases, handle those in the retrieval layer rather than corrupting the corpus with duplicates.

---

### 16. Preserve transliteration when available

For Sanskrit technical terms, retaining both Sanskrit and transliteration is useful.

Example:

क्षैप्रः (Kṣaipra)

प्रश्लिष्टः (Praśliṣṭa)

अभिनिहितः (Abhinihita)

जात्यस्वरितः (Jātyasvarita)

This helps when the user asks:

"Kṣaipra"

instead of:

"क्षैप्रः"

Your existing alias system provides additional protection, but clean transliteration in the corpus is still valuable.

---

### 17. Do not assume spelling variants are interchangeable

Sanskrit transliteration can appear in several forms.

For example:

Kṣaipra
Kshaipra
Ksaipra
क्षैप्र
क्षैप्रः

These may refer to the same technical term, but do not randomly replace the source's form.

Preserve the source form.

Handle common search variants through retrieval aliases when necessary.

---

### 18. Be careful with OCR

OCR is one of the biggest dangers when expanding a Sanskrit corpus.

For example, OCR may turn:

उदात्त

into:

उदात्त

or produce subtle errors in:

स्वरित
जात्यस्वरित
संधि
क्षैप्रः

Before adding OCR-derived material:

1. Inspect the Sanskrit.
2. Check verse boundaries.
3. Check diacritics/transliteration.
4. Check examples.
5. Check headings.
6. Remove obvious OCR artifacts.

Bad OCR can be worse than missing data because the RAG may confidently retrieve incorrect Sanskrit.

---

### 19. Remove irrelevant material

The corpus should contain material relevant to the tutor's intended domain.

For example, if the tutor is currently a Sanskrit grammar/Vedic-accent tutor, random unrelated material should not be added simply because it is Sanskrit.

GOOD:

Sandhi
Svaras
Samāsa
Pratyaya
Vedic accent
Grammar rules

Potentially unrelated:

A completely unrelated Sanskrit literary essay.

A larger corpus is not automatically a better corpus.

---

### 20. Preserve conflicting interpretations

If two sources disagree, DO NOT silently merge them.

Example:

SOURCE A:
Kṣaipra is defined as X.

SOURCE B:
Kṣaipra is interpreted as Y.

Keep both.

The tutor can then say:

"Source A states X, while Source B states Y."

This is much better than creating a fake single definition.

---

### 21. Preserve historical claims only when the source actually makes them

If a source says:

"According to Śākalya..."

preserve that attribution.

If the source does NOT say who originated a term, do not add:

"Kṣaipra was invented by..."

from external knowledge unless you are deliberately creating an externally researched source.

This is particularly important because your tutor is designed to be source-grounded.

---

### 22. Keep quotations distinguishable from explanations

GOOD:

SOURCE RULE:
उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य...

SOURCE TRANSLATION:
...

EXPLANATION:
In simple terms,...

This makes it possible for the model to distinguish:

"What the source literally states"

from:

"What an explanatory layer says."

---

### 23. Avoid excessive formatting

Plain text is preferable.

You do not need elaborate Markdown.

GOOD:

क्षैप्रः

Definition:
...

Example:
...

BAD:

############################
!!!!!!! KṢAIPRA !!!!!!!!!
############################

The corpus should be readable to both humans and the parser.

---

### 24. Use consistent labels

Across files, use consistent labels such as:

SOURCE:
SECTION:
SUBSECTION:
SANSKRIT:
TRANSLATION:
EXPLANATION:
EXAMPLE:
NOTE:

For example:

SOURCE: Svaramañjarī
SECTION: क्षैप्रः

SANSKRIT:
उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य...

TRANSLATION:
...

EXAMPLE:
त्रि + अंबकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

NOTE:
...

Consistency becomes increasingly valuable as the corpus grows.

---

### 25. Don't make chunks artificially tiny

Avoid turning every sentence into a separate retrieval document.

For grammar, a useful unit is generally:

HEADING
+
RULE
+
TRANSLATION
+
EXPLANATION
+
EXAMPLES

This is close to the rule-level chunking you are already using.

---

### 26. Don't make chunks enormous either

A whole 100-page book should not become one retrieval document.

Bad:

Book → one giant chunk.

Better:

Book
→ Chapter
→ Section
→ Subsection/rule
→ examples

The exact chunking can be adjusted during database creation.

---

### 27. Keep source boundaries clean

Prefer:

FILE 1:
One source/book/document

FILE 2:
Another source/book/document

rather than randomly combining unrelated books.

This makes source attribution and debugging much easier.

---

### 28. Before adding a new source, check for duplicates

If you already have:

जात्यस्वरितः

from Source A, and you are adding Source B containing the same section, do not assume it is redundant.

Instead ask:

* Is Source B actually the same text?
* Is it a commentary?
* Is it an alternative interpretation?
* Is it a different edition?
* Does it contain additional examples?

Duplicate source material can be removed; genuinely different interpretations should be preserved.

---

### 29. Maintain a corpus manifest

As the corpus grows, maintain a simple file such as:

corpus_manifest.txt

Example:

SOURCE 1:
File: svaramanjari.txt
Topic: Vedic Accent
Sections: Svara, Svarita, Jātyasvarita, Kampa

SOURCE 2:
File: paninian_grammar.txt
Topic: Pāṇinian Grammar
Sections: Sandhi, Samāsa, Pratyaya

SOURCE 3:
File: ...

This is extremely useful when debugging retrieval.

---

### 30. Test retrieval after major corpus expansion

Do NOT assume:

"More documents = better RAG."

After adding a significant amount of material, rerun your benchmark.

For example:

QUERY:
What is Kṣaipra?

Expected:
क्षैप्रः

QUERY:
What are the three Sandhi types producing Jātyasvarita?

Expected:
जात्योत्पत्तिः
क्षैप्रः
प्रश्लिष्टः
अभिनिहितः

QUERY:
What is the Sanskrit word for 10?

Expected:
If the expanded corpus still doesn't contain numerals:
"The retrieved source does not establish the answer."

---

### 31. Test both exact and natural-language queries

Test:

"क्षैप्रः"

"Kṣaipra"

"Kshaipra"

"What is Kṣaipra?"

"Which Sandhi produces Kṣaipra?"

"How is Jātyasvarita produced?"

The system should retrieve the appropriate material across these formulations.

---

### 32. Test questions that should NOT be answered

This is just as important as testing questions that should be answered.

Examples:

"Who invented Kṣaipra?"

"What is the Sanskrit word for 10?"

"What does Pāṇini 6.1.77 say?"

If the corpus doesn't establish these, the tutor should say so.

This prevents corpus expansion from accidentally turning the system into an unrestricted hallucinating chatbot.

---

### 33. Keep a benchmark set

Maintain a test file such as:

retrieval_benchmark.txt

Example:

TEST 1:
Question:
What is Kṣaipra?

Required sections:
क्षैप्रः

TEST 2:
Question:
What are the three Sandhi origins of Jātyasvarita?

Required sections:
जात्योत्पत्तिः
क्षैप्रः
प्रश्लिष्टः
अभिनिहितः

TEST 3:
Question:
Who invented Kṣaipra?

Expected:
Corpus does not establish the answer.

Every time the database is rebuilt, run this benchmark.

---

### 34. Separate corpus expansion from retrieval changes

When adding new material, don't simultaneously change:

* embedding model
* BM25 parameters
* RRF weights
* chunking
* aliases
* prompts

unless necessary.

Otherwise, if performance changes, you won't know why.

A good workflow is:

1. Expand corpus.
2. Clean corpus.
3. Validate corpus.
4. Rebuild database.
5. Run existing benchmark.
6. Compare results.
7. Only then modify retrieval if necessary.

---

### 35. Do not rebuild the database manually after every tiny edit

For several small corpus additions, collect them together.

Then:

CORPUS UPDATE
→ VALIDATE
→ REBUILD Chroma
→ RUN BENCHMARK
→ DEPLOY

This keeps the database reproducible.

---

# EXAMPLE

SOURCE:
Svaramañjarī

TOPIC:
Vedic Accent

SECTION:
क्षैप्रः (Kṣaipra)

SANSKRIT:

उदात्तस्यानुदात्तस्य यदा संधिर्यणात्मकः ।
क्षैप्रनामा तदा जात्यस्वरितः सर्वदा भवेत् ॥ २६॥

TRANSLATION:

If an Udātta and an Anudātta join to create a Yaṇ, the resulting Jātya Svarita is called Kṣaipra.

EXAMPLE:

त्रि + अंबकम् = त्र्यम्बकम्

# GOLDEN RULES

1. Preserve the original Sanskrit.
2. Use UTF-8.
3. Keep headings.
4. Keep rules, translations, explanations, and examples together.
5. Preserve parent-child relationships.
6. Identify every source.
7. Do not silently merge different sources.
8. Do not silently correct source material.
9. Do not mix your own explanation with source text without labeling it.
10. Preserve Sanskrit examples exactly.
11. Preserve transliteration where available.
12. Keep OCR errors out of the final corpus.
13. Avoid unnecessary duplication.
14. Do not turn everything into Q&A.
15. Keep chunks conceptually coherent.
16. Preserve conflicting interpretations rather than merging them.
17. Test retrieval after major corpus additions.
18. Test questions that should be answered AND questions that should be refused.
19. Keep a permanent retrieval benchmark.
20. Treat corpus quality as more important than corpus size.

# MOST IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE

The corpus should allow the RAG to answer:

"Where did this piece of information come from?"

For every important answer, the ideal chain is:

SOURCE
↓
SECTION
↓
RULE
↓
EXPLANATION
↓
EXAMPLE
↓
RETRIEVAL
↓
GROUNDED ANSWER

If you preserve that chain while expanding the corpus, your current retrieval architecture can scale much more reliably.
